SO-ARM101 / Imitation learning / Real-robot testing

Teaching a dual-arm robot to fold a handkerchief

Trained on 100 demonstrations, ACT folded a handkerchief into quarters in 37 of 40 trials under the same cloth and placement conditions. Failures with different patterns or positions show the limits we are investigating on the real robot.

Contents

  • ACT results and test conditions
  • Additional training and real-robot evaluation of π0.5

We are training and testing handkerchief-folding movements on a dual-arm SO-ARM101 robot. We collect demonstration data and check whether the trained model can grasp, lift, and fold cloth.

ACT results and test conditions

An ACT model trained on 100 demonstrations successfully folded the handkerchief into quarters in 37 out of 40 trials, using the same handkerchief and placement conditions as in training. However, it sometimes failed when the pattern or placement changed, revealing the challenge of handling different conditions.

Additional training and real-robot evaluation of π0.5

We are also continuing additional training and real-robot evaluation of π0.5. After revisiting the training process and the robot’s control conditions, we confirmed successful folding movements. Alongside numerical metrics during training, we observe actual movements and how failures unfold as we continue improving the model’s ability to perform the task reliably.
